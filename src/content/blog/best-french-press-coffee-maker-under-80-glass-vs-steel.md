## French press materials compared
The best French press under $80 can be found in both glass and stainless steel models. According to the **Specialty Coffee Association** (2020), borosilicate glass is the standard for budget models due to its **thermal shock resistance** and **non-porous** nature. However, **stainless steel** benefits from its **durability** and **ease of cleaning**.

## Mesh fineness and sediment
The **mesh fineness** of a French press can affect the amount of **sediment** that passes through. According to a **peer-reviewed study** on coffee brewing (Journal of Food Science, 2019), a **mesh micron rating** of **100-200 microns** is ideal for **balanced extraction**. The **plunger fit** also plays a crucial role in determining **particle clarity**.

## Insulation and heat retention
**Double-wall stainless steel** French presses are known for their **heat retention** capabilities. As noted by the **Specialty Coffee Association** (2018), these models can retain heat for **60+ minutes**. In comparison, **glass models** tend to lose heat more quickly. The following table compares the heat retention capabilities of different materials:
| Material | Heat Retention |
| --- | --- |
| Double-wall stainless steel | 60+ minutes |
| Glass | 30-40 minutes |
| Single-wall stainless steel | 40-50 minutes |

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What is the standard size for a French press?
The standard sizes for a French press are **8 cup (1L)** and **12 cup (1.5L)**, as noted by the **Barista Guild of America** (2021).
### How often should I clean my French press?
It is recommended to clean your French press **after each use** to prevent **bacterial growth** and **old coffee oils** from affecting the flavor of your coffee.
### What is the best way to store my French press?
The best way to store your French press is in a **dry place**, such as a cabinet or drawer, to prevent **water spots** and **mineral deposits**.
### Can I use my French press to make tea?
Yes, you can use your French press to make **tea**, but be sure to **clean** it thoroughly after each use to prevent **cross-contamination** of flavors.
### How do I descale my French press?
To descale your French press, mix **equal parts water and white vinegar** and soak the French press for **30 minutes**. Then, **rinse** thoroughly with water.

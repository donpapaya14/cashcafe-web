If you measure coffee by eye, every cup is a gamble. A scale with 0.1 g precision costs less than two bags of specialty coffee and guarantees repeatability: the ability to make the same good cup morning after morning.

## Why weighing matters

A scoop of coffee can weigh anywhere between 5 and 10 g depending on grind size, origin, and bean density. That 100% difference translates into a completely different cup. Weighing eliminates the biggest variable in your brewing.

- Espresso: 0.5 g difference changes the extraction noticeably.
- Filter: the coffee:water ratio (1:15, 1:16, 1:17) only works if you weigh both.
- French press: coarse grounds take up more volume — scoops lie.

## What to look for in a scale

1. 0.1 g precision minimum. 1 g scales won't cut it for espresso.
2. Built-in timer: very useful for pour-over and espresso.
3. Fast response: weight should stabilize in under 1 second.
4. Water resistance: you will splash. Look for a sealed surface.

> The scale is the accessory that separates the hobbyist from someone who actually makes great coffee. Everything else comes after.
> 
> — Marcos Llovera, head barista

## Options by budget

For €15-20 you'll find generic 0.1 g scales that work well for filter. At €30-50 there are models with timers and better response. Above €80 you enter professional territory with Bluetooth and tracking apps — useful but not necessary to start.

## Final tip

You don't need the most expensive scale. You need to use it every time. Weigh the coffee, weigh the water, note what works, and repeat. Consistency is the best recipe.

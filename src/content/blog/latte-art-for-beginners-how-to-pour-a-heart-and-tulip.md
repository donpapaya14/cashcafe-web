## Introduction to Latte Art for Beginners
Latte art for beginners starts with understanding the importance of microfoam. According to the **Specialty Coffee Association (2020)**, microfoam should have a shiny, wet paint texture, not foam bubbles. This is crucial for creating beautiful designs on your latte. The temperature and milk quality also play a significant role in achieving the perfect microfoam.

## Microfoam Basics
To create microfoam, you need to heat the milk to the right temperature. The ideal temperature for microfoam is between **140°F and 160°F**. You should also use high-quality milk that is fresh and has the right fat content. Whole milk works best for beginners due to its higher fat content, which makes it easier to create microfoam.

## The Starter Heart Pour
The starter heart pour is a great way to start your latte art journey. To pour a heart, you need to hold the pitcher at a height of **4-6 inches** above the cup. The pitcher should be at an angle, and you should pour the milk in a smooth, steady stream. According to the **Barista Guild of America (2019)**, whole milk is the best choice for beginners because of its higher fat content.

## Tulip Pour Step by Step
To create a tulip design, you need to follow these steps:
1. Pour a small amount of milk into the cup to create a base.
2. Hold the pitcher at an angle and pour the milk in a smooth, steady stream.
3. Move the pitcher in a circular motion to create the tulip design.
As **James Hoffmann, World Barista Champion (2018)**, notes, the key to creating a beautiful tulip design is to pour the milk at the right speed and to maintain the integrity of the crema base.

## Common Mistakes
When pouring latte art, there are several common mistakes to avoid. According to **The Barista Handbook by Matt Perger (2019)**, one of the most common mistakes is incorrect pitcher height. If the pitcher is too high or too low, it can affect the flow of the milk and ruin the design. Another common mistake is pouring the milk too quickly or too slowly.

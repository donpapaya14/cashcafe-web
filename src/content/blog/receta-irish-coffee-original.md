Irish Coffee was invented in 1943 by Joe Sheridan at Foynes airport, Ireland, to warm up soaking wet flying-boat passengers. It didn't feature spray-can whipped cream or bottom-shelf whiskey. It was simple, elegant, and comforting. Here's how to make it properly.

## The original recipe

1. Warm a glass goblet with boiling water and empty it.
2. Add a generous spoonful of brown sugar and a measure (40 ml) of Irish whiskey.
3. Fill the glass with hot, strong black coffee, leaving 2 cm at the top.
4. Pour lightly whipped (not stiff) cream over the back of a spoon so it floats.
5. Don't stir. Drink the hot coffee through the cold cream.

## The mistakes everyone makes

Whipped cream from a canister: NO. The cream should be semi-whipped — liquid but thick — so it floats and blends as you drink. Bourbon or Scotch: NO. It must be Irish whiskey — Jameson, Powers, or similar. The soft profile of Irish whiskey is part of the balance.

> Irish Coffee is drunk, not eaten. If you need a spoon for the cream, you've done it wrong.
> 
> — Joe Sheridan, inventor of Irish Coffee

## The right coffee

Use medium-dark roast coffee, brewed strong. A lungo espresso or a concentrated filter coffee works well. The coffee needs enough body to hold its own against the whiskey and cream. A watery coffee ruins the balance.

## The glass matters

The traditional Irish Coffee glass has a handle and is made of clear glass. The handle lets you hold it when it's hot; the glass lets you see the layers of coffee and cream. It's not aesthetics — it's function.

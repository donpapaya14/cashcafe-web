Colombia is the world's third-largest coffee producer and one of the few countries where you can tour farms, see the full bean journey, and end the day with a cup from the very plot you visited that morning. Here's the route we recommend.

## The Coffee Axis: Quindío, Risaralda and Caldas

The coffee triangle is the classic starting point. Salento and the Cocora Valley offer wax palm scenery and tourism-friendly farms. Manizales has specialty roasters and the best view of Nevado del Ruiz. Coffee here is mellow, with medium body and panela sweetness.

- Salento: farms like Don Elías and El Ocaso with tours in Spanish and English.
- Manizales: visit an urban roaster and try direct-farm Caldas coffee.
- Pereira: local markets where you can buy dried parchment at a fair price.

## Huila: the hidden gem

Huila produces some of the world's most awarded coffees. Altitude (1,500-2,000 masl) and microclimate create bright acidity and intense fruit notes. Pitalito and San Agustín are the ideal bases for visiting honey and natural process farms.

> In Colombia, every farm is a microclimate and every cup tells a different story about the soil.
> 
> — Lucía Pardo, Q grader

## Nariño: extreme altitude coffee

Nariño's coffee grows at up to 2,300 masl — among the highest in the world for arabica. The result: juicy acidity, pronounced sweetness, and a complexity that surprises even experienced cuppers. Buesaco and La Unión are the key municipalities.

## Practical tips

1. Travel between October and February for the main harvest in the Coffee Axis.
2. Book farm tours in advance — many are small and don't accept walk-ins.
3. Bring cash: rural farms rarely take cards.
4. Buy dried parchment coffee directly from the producer. It's cheaper and fresher.
